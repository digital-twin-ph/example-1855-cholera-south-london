Snow's South London water supply study, 1854
============================================

Source:   John Snow, On the Mode of Communication of Cholera, second
          edition, London: John Churchill, 1855. Tables VII, VIII, IX
          and XII, Map 2, and the first-four-weeks figures in the
          surrounding text.

Obtained: Project Gutenberg eBook #72894, release date 7 February 2024,
          produced from scanned images of public domain material from
          the Google Books project by Richard Tonsing, MWS and the
          Online Distributed Proofreading Team, with maps provided by
          the UCLA Fielding School of Public Health website.
          https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/72894

Licence:  None applies to the figures. The publication is from 1855 and
          is in the public domain, and the figures are facts rather than
          expression. The transcription in data/ is dedicated to the
          public domain under CC0 1.0 (see LICENSE), so nothing here
          adds a restriction that the source did not carry.

          This was a deliberate choice of source. The same figures are
          available inside licensed packages -- HistData is GPL,
          causaldata is MIT -- and taking them from a digitisation would
          have adopted that digitisation's terms for data that is
          otherwise unencumbered. Transcribing from the original inherits
          nothing.

See provenance.json for the digest of the text transcribed from, the
three separate dates these figures carry, and the limits that apply to
any analysis built on them.


THE PLATE ITSELF
================

geo/map2.jpg and geo/map2-large.jpg are Snow's Map 2, the plate this study is argued
from: the boundaries of the Registrar-General's districts south of the Thames, with
the water supply of those districts shown as a colour wash.

Source:   Project Gutenberg eBook #72894
          https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/72894/images/i_map2.jpg
          https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/72894/images/i_map2_large.jpg
Licence:  None applies. The plate is from 1855 and is in the public domain; Project
          Gutenberg asserts no rights over a scan of a public-domain work.
Sizes:    1002 x 788 and 5000 x 3934. Both are the same plate, so both share the
          georeference in geo/map2-georeference.json; map2.jgw places the small one
          and map2-large.jgw the large.

Stored here on 2026-10-27, having been georeferenced on 2026-10-24 with only its URL
and digests recorded. Both files match those digests exactly.

Worth recording because it nearly went wrong: two successive downloads of the large
plate were silently truncated -- 972 kB and then 495 kB of a 5,341,773-byte file --
and an image tool read the truncated file as a perfectly good 5000 x 3934 JPEG. Only
the recorded digest said otherwise.

geo/map2-water-supply-zones.geojson is the colour wash traced from that plate, as
three polygons: the Southwark and Vauxhall territory in blue, the Lambeth territory in
red, and in purple the area where both companies' pipes are intermingled. The wash is
a supply TERRITORY and not the course of any pipe, and the purple zone is the whole of
Snow's design.

OPENSTREETMAP FILES
===================

geo/thames-reaches.geojson and geo/thames-tidal-limit.geojson are NOT part of this
repository's CC0 dedication. They are derived from OpenStreetMap and are therefore
licensed under the Open Database License, which is SHARE-ALIKE: a work derived from
them must be offered under the same terms.

Source:   OpenStreetMap contributors, via the Geofabrik "greater-london" extract
          (greater-london.osm.pbf, 129,796,300 bytes, retrieved 2026-10-22).
          https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
Licence:  Open Database License v1.0 (ODbL). The data is (c) OpenStreetMap
          contributors. Any Produced Work must credit them; any Derivative
          Database must be offered under the ODbL.
          https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/

What was taken, and what was done to it:

  Seven water-area multipolygon relations -- 28934, 308126, 318374, 2110237,
  3295636, 4028190 and 16570637 -- assembled from their member ways into closed
  rings, PARTITIONED into two reaches, then CLIPPED to the study area's bounding
  box. Nothing was simplified or reprojected and no vertex was moved; six of the
  seven relations survive the clip, and relation 2110237, the reach above Thames
  Ditton at Hampton and Molesey, falls outside the box entirely. Each reach
  records which relations it kept and which were clipped away.

  THE BOX IS THE STUDY AREA'S, with a 5,000 m margin, which is how acquisition is
  already scoped in this application: set_bbox(geometryBounds(boundary) +/- marginM).
  The 32 sub-districts lie inside it by construction, a polygon being within its own
  bounds. The margin is 5,000 m rather than the 2,000 m that osmQuery permits because
  Teddington Lock needs 4,460 m and Thames Ditton 4,833 m: that cap bounds an Overpass
  REQUEST, and street-engine.ts applies its margin uncapped because reading a local
  extract costs a file read instead. The clip was made here rather than in a workflow
  step, because no widget clips polygons -- clip_points_to_area takes points. So this
  clip has no receipt inside the application and is recorded in this file and in each
  feature's own properties instead.

  THE DIVISION IS OPENSTREETMAP'S OWN, which is the point worth recording. The
  intention was to cut one waterway polygon at the tidal limit. It proved
  unnecessary: relations 308126 and 318374 already SHARE 16 VERTICES spanning 194 m
  -- the width of the river -- 388 m from the Teddington Lock point this repository
  carries. OpenStreetMap divides the water area at the lock, because a lock is a
  structure that interrupts it. So geo/thames-tidal-limit.geojson is that shared
  edge, copied, not a line drawn here; and the two reaches are the relations joined
  to either side of it, found by shared vertices alone.

  One relation, 16570637, touches neither side. It is placed in the non-tidal reach
  by its nearest centreline node, 1003 m away and tagged tidal=no. That single
  assignment rests on proximity rather than on topology.

  Relation 318374 covers Teddington to Richmond and carries NO tidal tag, while
  relation 28934 below it carries tidal=yes. The absent tag was at first read as
  OpenStreetMap placing the limit 3403 m downstream, at Richmond. It is not: it is
  a missing tag, and the reach is placed by what it is joined to.

Vintage:  THESE ARE MODERN BANKS, and the study is of 1854. The Victoria Embankment
          narrowed the central river from 1865, so the tidal reach drawn here is not
          the one Snow's intakes stood on. The reaches carry the tidal/non-tidal
          DISTINCTION faithfully and the river's 1854 shape not at all. A machined
          note records this under Discussion as a limitation.

Checked:  The three water-works points this repository carries each fall on the side
          the repository claims -- Southwark and Vauxhall at Battersea Fields and
          Lambeth at Hungerford in the tidal reach, Lambeth at Thames Ditton in the
          non-tidal -- which is an independent test of a claim the README records as
          this repository's own, since Snow names Teddington but no boundary.
